## 1. Executive Summary

RetailFlow is a proposed digital solution designed to help small retail businesses manage products, inventory, sales and basic business performance information from one simple system.

Many small businesses still rely on notebooks, spreadsheets, messaging applications or disconnected records to track stock and sales. These approaches can make it difficult to know current stock levels, identify low-stock products, understand sales performance and maintain reliable records.

The RetailFlow project will design, develop, test and pilot a lightweight Minimum Viable Product (MVP) that provides:

- Product catalogue management
- Inventory and stock movement tracking
- Sales recording
- Low-stock alerts
- Basic management dashboards and reports
- Controlled user access
- User training and pilot support

The project will use a **Hybrid Agile** approach. Formal project governance will be used for scope, budget, milestones, approvals and release decisions, while Agile iterations will be used for requirements refinement, design, development, testing and user feedback.

The project is planned for **14 calendar weeks**, from 12 October 2026 to 15 January 2027, with an authorised budget of **₦8,950,500**.

## 2. Project Charter

### 2.1 Project Purpose

To design, develop, test and pilot a simple and affordable digital inventory and sales management system for small retailers.

### 2.2 Business Case / Justification

Small retailers need accurate and accessible information about their products, stock and sales to support day-to-day decision-making.

RetailFlow will address this need by creating a centralised digital system that reduces dependence on manual records and provides users with clearer information about business activity.

**Expected business value includes:**

- Improved inventory visibility
- Better sales record keeping
- Faster identification of low-stock products
- Reduced dependence on manual calculations
- Improved access to basic business information
- Better support for operational decision-making
- A foundation for future digital expansion

The project is intentionally focused on an MVP rather than attempting to create a full enterprise resource planning platform.

### 2.3 Project Objectives

The project will:

1. Establish and approve clear business and system requirements.
2. Design an intuitive user experience for the core retail workflows.
3. Develop an MVP covering products, inventory, sales, alerts and reporting.
4. Test the system against agreed acceptance criteria.
5. Train nominated pilot users.
6. Deploy the solution to a controlled pilot environment.
7. Obtain user feedback and acceptance.
8. Complete project handover and closure by 15 January 2027.

## 13. Risk Management Plan

Risk management will follow a continuous process:  
**Identify → Analyse → Plan Response → Assign Owner → Monitor → Escalate/Close**

### Risk Register

| ID | Risk | Probability | Impact | Rating | Response |
|----|------|-------------|--------|--------|----------|
| R1 | Requirements change/scope creep | Medium | High | High | Baseline requirements and enforce change control |
| R2 | Development delays | Medium | High | High | Short iterations, dependency tracking and technical spikes |
| R3 | Critical defects near go-live | Medium | High | High | Continuous testing and release gates |
| R4 | Low user adoption | Medium | High | High | User involvement, usability testing and training |
| R5 | Poor-quality business data | Medium | Medium | Medium | Data validation, sample migration and reconciliation |
| R6 | Hosting/service outage | Low | High | Medium | Reliable hosting, backups and recovery procedures |
| R7 | Security/privacy weakness | Low | High | Medium | Access controls, secure credentials and security testing |
| R8 | Vendor/resource unavailability | Medium | Medium | Medium | Documentation, cross-training and alternative resources |

Each risk will have a named owner responsible for monitoring triggers, implementing responses and escalating when necessary. The RAID log will be reviewed at least weekly.

### 13.1 Issue Management

Issues (problems that have already occurred) will be managed separately from risks:

1. Log the issue in the RAID log with owner, impact and target resolution date.
2. Assess urgency and impact.
3. Assign resolution actions.
4. Track to closure.
5. Escalate if resolution is blocked or exceeds agreed tolerance.

**Issue vs Risk distinction** is maintained so that proactive risk responses are not confused with reactive problem-solving.

## 16. Change-Control Approach

Changes will be controlled to prevent uncontrolled scope, schedule and budget growth.

**Change Process**

1. Raise and log the change request.
2. Clarify the requested change and reason.
3. Analyse impact on: Scope, Cost, Schedule, Quality, Risk, Resources.
4. Submit the change to the appropriate decision authority.
5. Approve, reject or defer the request.
6. Update approved baselines where necessary.
7. Implement and test approved changes.
8. Communicate the decision.
9. Close the change request with evidence.

No significant change should be implemented solely through informal verbal agreement.

### 16.1 Escalation & Tolerance Thresholds

| Area | Tolerance | Escalation |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Cost (work package) | ±5% | Project Manager investigates; >10% requires Sponsor |
| Schedule (milestone) | 3 working days | Project Manager; >5 days requires Sponsor decision |
| Scope | Any material change | Formal change request to Sponsor / Steering |
| Quality (Critical defects) | Zero open Critical defects at Go-Live | Sponsor acceptance required if exception sought |

## 20. Development Approach

**Hybrid Agile**

RetailFlow will combine predictive governance with Agile delivery.

- **Predictive Elements** used for: Project charter, Scope baseline, Budget approval, Governance, Major milestones, Formal acceptance, Go-live approval
- **Agile Elements** used for: Requirements refinement, UX/UI development, Product backlog, Incremental development, Iteration reviews, User feedback, Testing, Retrospectives

A purely predictive approach could make it difficult to respond efficiently to user feedback during development. A purely Agile approach may provide insufficient structure for budget, governance and formal academic project controls. The hybrid approach therefore provides structure at project level while retaining flexibility at delivery level.
